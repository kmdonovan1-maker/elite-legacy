The Research Behind Engagement

The experiences we create aren't just enjoyable. They matter.

Conversation. Movement. Games. Cooking. Creativity. Hobbies. Getting outside. Learning. Remembering. Simply being with someone.

Research from respected institutions continues to show that staying socially, mentally and physically engaged can matter to health and wellbeing as we age.

Why We Pay Attention

The science informs what we do. The person tells us how to do it.

Elite Legacy is not a medical service and our Companions do not provide medical treatment.

But we believe families deserve to understand why the experiences we create matter.

The following research comes from organizations including the National Institute on Aging, the U.S. Surgeon General and Cochrane — respected sources of information on aging, health and wellbeing.

01 — SOCIAL CONNECTION

Connection and cognitive health

~50%

The U.S. Surgeon General's advisory reports that chronic loneliness and social isolation among older adults can increase the risk of developing dementia by approximately 50%.

Source: U.S. Surgeon General — Advisory on the Healing Effects of Social Connection
02 — FALL PREVENTION

Exercise and falls

23% fewer falls

A Cochrane systematic review found that exercise reduced the rate of falls among older adults living in the community by approximately 23%. It also reduced the number of people experiencing one or more falls by approximately 15%.

Source: Cochrane Review — Exercise for preventing falls in older people living in the community
03 — PHYSICAL FUNCTION

Movement and independence

The National Institute on Aging reports that physical activity can improve physical function and make everyday tasks easier, supporting the ability of older adults to remain independent.

Source: National Institute on Aging — Health Benefits of Exercise and Physical Activity
04 — MOOD

Physical activity and emotional wellbeing

The National Institute on Aging reports that physical activity can help reduce feelings of depression and anxiety, improve sleep and benefit overall emotional wellbeing.

Source: National Institute on Aging — Health Benefits of Exercise and Physical Activity
05 — COGNITIVE ENGAGEMENT

An engaged mind

Research discussed by the National Institute on Aging suggests that cognitive training and social engagement can benefit aspects of cognitive health.

The evidence does not establish that any single activity prevents dementia. What it does demonstrate is the importance of continuing to engage the mind.

Source: National Institute on Aging — Cognitive Health and Older Adults
06 — PURPOSE & MEANING

Having something meaningful to do

The National Institute on Aging describes meaningful activities as an important part of healthy aging and notes associations with wellbeing, happiness, life satisfaction and resilience.

Source: National Institute on Aging — Participating in Activities You Enjoy As You Age
An Important Distinction

We don't make medical promises.

Research can show associations and benefits. It cannot tell us exactly what will happen to one individual.

Elite Legacy does not diagnose, treat or prevent medical conditions. Our Companions are not healthcare providers.

We simply believe that an engaged life is worth pursuing — and that the research gives us good reason to take connection, movement, mental engagement and meaningful activity seriously.

And Then There Is Something Else

Something research can't completely measure.

Does this person look forward to seeing us again?

Because when someone wakes up knowing that a person they enjoy is coming later that day, the day already has something in it.

Sometimes the greatest benefit isn't something that can be captured in a medical study.

It's simply having a really good day.
