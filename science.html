The Science Behind a More Engaged Life

The science informs what we do.
The person tells us how to do it.

We intuitively know that conversation, movement, laughter, curiosity, purpose and having something to look forward to can make a day feel better.

Research increasingly shows that these things matter in ways that go well beyond simply having a good day.

Social connection, physical activity, mental engagement and a sense of purpose have all been studied for their relationship to healthy aging, cognitive health, emotional well-being and quality of life.

We aren't trying to turn someone's day into a health program. We're helping them live an interesting, engaged and meaningful life — and there is remarkable science behind why that matters.

01 — SOCIAL CONNECTION

Isolation and cognitive health

27% higher risk

A Johns Hopkins-led study reported an association between social isolation and a 27% higher risk of developing dementia over nine years among older adults.

Source: Johns Hopkins research summarized by Wisconsin Alzheimer's Disease Research Center — view source
02 — FALL PREVENTION

Exercise and falls

23% fewer falls

A Cochrane systematic review found that exercise reduced the rate of falls among older adults living in the community by approximately 23%.

Source: Cochrane Review — view review
03 — SOCIAL HEALTH

Connection reaches beyond loneliness

The U.S. Surgeon General's advisory identifies social disconnection as a meaningful public-health concern associated with risks affecting cardiovascular health, depression, anxiety and cognitive health.

Source: U.S. Surgeon General, 2023 Advisory — view advisory
04 — MOVEMENT

Activity supports function

Research on healthy aging consistently supports appropriate physical activity as part of maintaining mobility, function, confidence and independence.

Source: National Institute on Aging — physical activity and healthy aging guidance.
05 — COGNITIVE ENGAGEMENT

An engaged mind

No single game, hobby or conversation can promise to prevent dementia. But continued cognitive and social engagement are repeatedly studied as parts of healthy aging.

Source: National Institute on Aging — cognitive health and older adults.
06 — PURPOSE & MEANING

Having something to do tomorrow

Meaningful activities can support wellbeing, identity and a sense of purpose. For Elite Legacy, that can be as simple as a recipe to finish, a walk to take, a story to write or a person someone is looking forward to seeing.

Source: National Institute on Aging — participation in meaningful activities.
A Note About the Research

Research adds perspective.
The experience remains human.

Elite Legacy is a companionship and lifestyle experience, not a medical service. Our Companions do not diagnose or treat medical conditions.

The research shared here is part of a larger body of evidence showing why connection, movement, curiosity, purpose and meaningful activity deserve an important place in our lives as we age. Research describes associations and population-level findings; it cannot predict what will happen for any one individual.
