About Elite Legacy

This didn't begin as a business idea. It began with a realization.

What would I want my own life to look like when I reach this stage?

Many of us eventually find ourselves watching our parents grow older. Sometimes we've already lost one of them.

We remember what that was like. The changes. The smaller world. The days that seemed to have less in them.

And then, almost without realizing it, we begin wondering about our own future.

What will my life look like then?
Who will I spend my days with?
What will I have to look forward to?

Those questions stayed with me.

And then came the slightly selfish part.

When I get to this stage of my own life, this is what I would want.

I wouldn't want someone simply checking on me. I wouldn't want someone whose job was just to make sure I was okay.

I'd want someone interesting to talk to. Someone who would listen to my stories. Someone who would ask questions. Someone who would laugh with me. Someone who would want to know what I was thinking.

I'd want someone who would say, "What do you feel like doing today?" and actually be interested in the answer.

I would want someone who was interested in who I still am — not simply what I can no longer do.

And that's when the idea of Elite Legacy began to take shape.

The Idea

Not care. Connection.

Elite Legacy isn't a traditional home-care service. It isn't a babysitting service. And it isn't about filling eight hours with activities.

It is about creating a genuine human relationship around the person — their interests, their experiences, their stories, their humor, their curiosity and the things that still make them feel alive.

Because people don't stop being interesting because they get older.

Starting Carefully

We're beginning with a very small number of families. On purpose.

The first relationships will help establish the standards, practices and training that will eventually allow Elite Legacy to bring this experience to more people.

Every relationship will teach us something. Every Companion will be trained in what we learn.

We're not trying to become large quickly.

We're trying to become exceptional first.

The Reason

Maybe the greatest gift we can give someone is something to look forward to.

A person coming through the door. A favorite game. A walk. A conversation. A project. A story they haven't told anyone in years.

Something that makes tomorrow feel a little more interesting.
