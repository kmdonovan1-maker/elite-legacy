What We Believe

People don't stop being interesting because they get older.

They still have opinions, humor, curiosity, stories, favorite foods, old passions, new ideas and things they have always wanted to do.

Somewhere along the way, we began confusing being cared for with having a life. Meals matter. Safety matters. Help matters. But so does laughing. Having plans for Thursday. Being asked the next question. Having something to look forward to tomorrow.

Safe isn't the finish line. A life still needs something in it.
What Could Tomorrow Look Like?

Not an activity list. A really good day.

Maybe Dad mentions that he used to make an incredible Bolognese.

His Companion doesn't say, “That's nice.”

He says, “Then why aren't we making it?”

Tomorrow they're at the market buying ingredients. While they're cooking, Dad starts talking about the first apartment he and Mom had. His Companion asks where it was. One story becomes another.

Somewhere along the way they decide those stories should be written down.

Now they're making dinner and beginning Dad's Legacy Life Story.

Nobody scheduled a “cognitive engagement activity.” They were simply having a good day — and creating something worth continuing tomorrow.
